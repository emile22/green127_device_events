UC 2.12 is granular enough because the text gives **explicit operational states, triggers, and reconfiguration actions**, not just a high-level goal.

The strongest evidence is that it already describes a natural event sequence:

- peak traffic → **high-resolution sensing**
- low traffic → **low-power sensing**
- context change → **some functions activated/deactivated**
- energy optimization → **traffic paths rerouted**
- critical functions → **kept continuously active**

The draft says critical functions such as threat detection must run continuously, while others should activate according to context. It also explicitly says the system can switch between high-resolution and low-power sensing modes, turn components off or put them in sleep/standby, adjust sensing resolution or bandwidth, steer traffic, and coordinate globally. [GitHub](https://github.com/emile22/draft-ietf-green-use-cases/blob/main/draft-ietf-green-use-cases.md)

That gives us concrete event boundaries such as:

```text
U12-0 PEAK_HIGH_RESOLUTION_SENSING
U12-1 LOW_TRAFFIC_DETECTED
U12-2 SENSING_LOW_POWER
U12-3 ENERGY_AWARE_TRAFFIC_REROUTE
U12-4 CRITICAL_SENSING_PRESERVED
```

These are not arbitrary inventions; they are direct operationalizations of the draft’s own transitions:

```text
high-resolution sensing
        ↓
low traffic
        ↓
low-power mode
        ↓
traffic/path reconfiguration
        ↓
critical sensing remains active
```

The UC also gives enough device granularity for a simulator: it names roadside units, integrated sensors, transceivers, per-component energy measurement, per-link/per-mode reporting, and cross-domain aggregation. [GitHub](https://github.com/emile22/draft-ietf-green-use-cases/blob/main/draft-ietf-green-use-cases.md)

So compared with UC 2.3 or 2.4:

```text
UC 2.12
has explicit modes + triggers + actions + constraints
=> event-ready

UC 2.3 / 2.4
mostly describe reporting needs or desired observability
=> not enough concrete transition logic yet
```

The only caveat is that **the exact event names are ours**, not standardized by the draft. The underlying transitions are supported by the text.

**UC 2.3 — Reporting on Lifecycle Management** is deferred because the draft gives useful **data categories**, but not a concrete operational sequence.

It specifies things such as manufacturing energy cost, transport, recyclability, end-of-life impact, location-dependent carbon factors, energy mix, backup systems, and ingestion from vendor databases or datasheets. Those are primarily reporting inputs or relatively static metadata. The text does not define a trigger/action flow such as “condition detected → device changes state → new telemetry appears → recovery.” [GitHub](https://github.com/emile22/draft-ietf-green-use-cases/blob/main/draft-ietf-green-use-cases.md)

So for **UC 2.3 — Reporting on Lifecycle Management**, we could easily invent events like:

```text
DATASHEET_IMPORTED
CARBON_FACTOR_UPDATED
GRID_SWITCHED_TO_BATTERY
GENERATOR_STARTED
```

but only the underlying concepts are in the draft; that exact event progression is not. For a simulator meant to stay close to the UC text, that would require more interpretation than the Group A cases.

There is one important nuance: **UC 2.3 — Reporting on Lifecycle Management** is not “empty.” The draft explicitly requires awareness of backup systems and vendor/database ingestion, and even says the system should know when backup power is in use. [GitHub](https://github.com/emile22/draft-ietf-green-use-cases/blob/main/draft-ietf-green-use-cases.md) So it could become Group A later if we agree on a specific reporting/event model.
